# Portfolio reference review

Reviewed 20 international professional and creative portfolios, plus the reference site Anup Luintel shared in this conversation. These are examples selected for useful patterns, not a ranked list of the world's objectively best websites. The personal and SEO sites are more directly relevant to the copy; creative portfolios were included for storytelling and presentation patterns.

## The user-provided reference

[Anup Luintel](https://anupluintel.com/) is included separately because Rajesh explicitly shared it. Its “The Story Behind the Strategy” section gives the About area a point of view, relates the author's background to his working method, and connects biography to the service offer. Its service taxonomy and CMS/platform list also make breadth easy to understand. The long homepage, repeated proof points, conversion-heavy language, and large set of niche pages are specific to Anup's consultancy and should not be copied into Rajesh's profile.

## What fits Rajesh's profile

The current heading “About Rajesh Tudu” repeats the name already shown in the identity and navigation. It labels the section but does not preview its useful content. A better heading should introduce Rajesh's way of approaching SEO, while the copy supplies a concise, verifiable career arc and explains the relationship between semantic/entity research and the wider SEO disciplines.

The revised section uses **“The thinking behind my SEO work.”** It starts with the user's documented progression from keyword research, on-page work, and reporting, then explains how that experience informs a broader practice across on-page, off-page, local, technical, and e-commerce SEO on different CMS platforms. It mentions performance data and small Python research tools without claiming unsupported outcomes or repeating the hero paragraph verbatim.

## Research and copy principles

- A descriptive heading should preview the section's substance, not merely repeat the person's name.
- Give the introduction one job: explain the professional point of view and the audience benefit.
- Give the About section a different job: explain how that point of view developed and how it informs current work.
- Keep a real career path, methods, and evidence specific to Rajesh. Do not borrow another site's biography, statistics, client claims, or distinctive wording.
- Keep examples that rely on unusual art, 3D, or game navigation as inspiration only where the interaction helps visitors understand Rajesh's work.
- Use Google Search Central's advice for helpful, accurate headings and natural, readable copy. Google does not prescribe a particular font, headline formula, or portfolio design.
